REFERENCE

10 notes for calendar makers

CGS
2027
01How a printable calendar grid works+

A month grid is an alignment of real dates into seven weekday columns. The first date's weekday determines the leading empty cells; the month length determines where the final row ends. Blank cells are layout space, not dates from a neighboring month.

02Gregorian leap-year rules+

A year is a leap year when divisible by 4, except a century year is not leap unless divisible by 400. This keeps the calendar aligned with the solar year over long intervals. For example, 2000 and 2400 are leap years; 1900 and 2100 are not.

03Why some months need six rows+

A Sunday-first or Monday-first grid needs six rows when the offset before day 1 plus the number of days in the month exceeds 35. February with a Saturday start in a leap year is one example; some 31-day months also need six rows.

04ISO week numbers, in plain terms+

ISO 8601 numbers weeks from Monday through Sunday. Week 1 is the week containing the year's first Thursday (equivalently, January 4). As a result, the ISO week-year can differ from the Gregorian year around New Year.

05A4 and US Letter are different proportions+

A4 is 210 × 297 mm, with a longer portrait proportion. US Letter is 8.5 × 11 inches (215.9 × 279.4 mm), slightly wider and shorter. Select the paper that matches the printer stock to avoid unexpected scaling.

06Portrait and landscape layouts+

Portrait lends a tall month view with room for notes below or beside the grid. Landscape gives more horizontal writing space and often larger day cells. The same date data can use either orientation.

07Typography and date readability+

Use clear numeral shapes, sufficient size and strong contrast. Keep weekday labels distinct from dates. A small amount of whitespace around numbers usually improves scanning more than heavier rules do.

08Margins, scaling and printer limits+

Most home printers cannot print to the very edge. Leave a safe margin and, in the browser print dialog, choose the same paper size as the design. “Fit to printable area” can shrink a page; “Actual size” preserves its intended dimensions when the printer supports them.

09Common PDF layout mistakes+

Typical problems include a paper-size mismatch, automatic headers and footers, browser scaling, clipped margins and background colors omitted by the print dialog. Review the print preview and turn off browser-added headers if they duplicate the calendar's own text.

10Verify before you print+

Check the first and last dates, count the days, confirm the weekday alignment and inspect any ISO week labels. Test a year transition and one leap-year February. Then compare the preview with the selected paper and orientation.
